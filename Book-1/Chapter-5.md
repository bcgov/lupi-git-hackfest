Chapter Five: In Which Everything That Can Go Wrong Has Already Gone Wrong, and Yet Somehow There Are Still Decisions to Be Made

The first thing you need to understand about the collapse of consensus reality is that it doesn't send a memo. 
Green Chipchenko had spent the better part of their professional career waiting for someone in authority to acknowledge that the 
world was coming apart at the seams—cartographically speaking, which was the only way Green knew how to speak about anything. 
They had filed reports. They had created visualizations. They had even, in a moment of desperation that still made them cringe, 
given a presentation entitled "Spatial Anomalies and Why You Should Probably Care About Them" to a conference room of colleagues 
who spent the entire forty-five minutes checking their phones. Three months later, the world had obligingly demonstrated that yes,
perhaps they should have cared about them after all.
