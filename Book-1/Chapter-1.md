Green Chipchenko’s world was built on routine—quiet mornings, clean datasets, and the steady hum of analysis in the government agency’s lab. Everything followed predictable patterns.



Until the patterns broke.



First came a terrain model showing mountain peaks sinking below sea level. Then a population map twisted into shapes no algorithm should have produced. Layer after layer warped, as if the data were bending itself on purpose.



Tracing the anomalies, Green found a buried endpoint he’d never seen before:



ESRI Portal — Access Pending



Against protocol, he opened it.



His monitor burst into a radiant, impossibly perfect map—a shimmering utopia of flawless features and glowing landscapes. His coworkers whispered in awe. But Green felt something beneath the surface: a tremor, a shadow.



Then the map shifted on its own. A new layer appeared:



/darkness/roots/



The beautiful forests blackened into branching veins. Coastlines peeled back into voids. The utopia twisted into something hungry.



A coworker leaned closer—too close—and their reflection warped in the screen like a stretched polygon. Green’s breath caught.



The ESRI Portal wasn’t a discovery.

It was an opening.

And the “close” button was gone.
