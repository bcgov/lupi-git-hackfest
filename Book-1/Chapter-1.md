Chapter 1 — The Quiet Coordinates

Green Chipchenko was the kind of geospatial analyst who spoke mostly through the soft clacking of his keyboard. In the basement-level Situation Analysis Lab of a government agency whose name changed slightly depending on which department funded it, Green was one node in a small team responsible for examining satellite imagery, vector layers, and obscure GIS data that rarely saw daylight.

Every day followed a ritual:
07:30—badge in.
07:40—coffee thick enough to patch asphalt.
07:50—update raster datasets.
08:00–16:30—map, model, analyze, repeat.

He liked it this way. The world made sense in grids, shapefiles, and coordinate systems. Human noise was minimal; the maps were predictable. The team worked quietly around him—people like Rina Holt, who asked too many questions, and Markus Benne, who listened to too few—but Green kept to himself.
